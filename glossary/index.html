Glossary

The vocabulary, in plain language.

No definition here assumes you already know three other terms first.

Algae, BBA (black beard algae)
A dark, bristly algae that grows on slow-flow areas and leaf edges — usually a sign of unstable or low CO2 rather than "too much light" on its own. See CO2 & lighting basics.
Aquascape
An aquarium arranged as a deliberate landscape — hardscape and plants composed as a scene, not just a habitat for fish.
Biotope
A tank built to accurately replicate one specific real-world location's water, substrate, hardscape, and species. See the Biotope style breakdown.
Carpet plant
A low-growing foreground species (dwarf hairgrass, Monte Carlo, glosso) planted densely to form a lawn-like ground cover.
Dry start method (DSM)
Growing carpet or emersed plants in a tank with no standing water for several weeks before flooding, so roots establish faster than they would fully submerged.
Drop checker
A small glass device that changes color (yellow to blue-green to blue) to indicate dissolved CO2 level — the standard way to gauge CO2 without a direct sensor.
Dutch streets
The straight or gently curved rows of a single plant species that make up a Dutch-style layout, each contrasting the next in color, texture, and height. See the Dutch style breakdown.
EI dosing (Estimative Index)
A fertilizer-dosing method that deliberately overdoses macro and micro nutrients on a weekly schedule, relying on a large weekly water change to reset levels rather than precise testing.
Emersed
Grown with leaves above the waterline rather than fully submerged — most aquatic plants grow faster and hardier this way, which is why the dry start method works.
Flow
The movement of water through a tank from filter output and any additional powerheads — even distribution prevents dead spots where CO2 and nutrients don't reach.
Hardscape
The non-living structural elements of a layout — stone and driftwood — placed before any planting begins. See hardscape placement.
Iwagumi
A minimalist style built from an odd number of stones (usually 3–5) and a single low-carpet plant. Japanese for "rock formation." See the Iwagumi style breakdown.
Nano tank
A small aquarium, generally under 10 gallons — often the entry point for aquascaping because it's cheap to experiment on and quick to rescape.
Negative space
Deliberately empty area in a layout — open substrate or water column left unplanted to give the eye somewhere to rest and the hardscape room to read.
Oyaishi
The "parent stone" in an Iwagumi layout — the largest stone, placed off-center, that every other stone's angle and size relates back to.
Paludarium
A tank that combines land and water in the same enclosure, usually connected by a slope or waterfall. See the Paludarium style breakdown.
Photoperiod
The number of hours per day the tank light runs — typically 6–8 hours for a planted tank.
Rhizome
A horizontal stem structure (found in anubias, java fern, bucephalandra) that must stay above the substrate — burying it causes rot.
Rule of thirds
A composition guideline placing the focal point at roughly one-third of the tank's width, rather than dead center.
Stem plant
A plant that grows as a vertical stem with leaves along its length (rotala, ludwigia) — typically planted in the background and trimmed regularly to control height.
Wabi-kusa
A ball of aquatic soil packed with plants and moss, grown emersed before being dropped into a tank — a shortcut to an already-established look on day one.
Walstad method
A low-tech, soil-based planted tank approach (named for Diana Walstad) that leans on plant mass and a natural nutrient cycle instead of CO2 injection or heavy dosing.
